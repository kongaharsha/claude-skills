# WK Presentation Storyline

Create a source-grounded storyline that enables a senior audience to make a specific decision. Produce a Word document by default; if a document is not requested, provide the same slide-by-slide structure in chat.

## Frame the decision

Establish the presentation objective, target audience, decision required, executive takeaway, source set, explicit exclusions, tone, and level of detail. Ask only when a missing item would materially change the narrative. Do not manufacture research, targets, customer claims, dates, figures, or commitments; label missing support `[SOURCE REQUIRED]`.

Develop a hypothesis-led narrative: begin with the answer or recommendation when the evidence supports one, then supply the minimum context, evidence, choices, and action needed for the decision. Use an action/assertion title for every slide—not a topic label. Separate facts, assumptions, and recommendations where they could be confused.

## AI Content Discipline

When the subject is AI, Agentic PDLC, or automation, distinguish the technology from the changed work. Do not use generic phrases such as "using AI," "AI-powered," "AI-enabled," "leverage AI," or "AI transformation" as a substitute for the actual mechanism. State what the team did differently, what artifact or workflow changed, and what evidence resulted. Use AI terminology only where it is material to the decision or accurately names a confirmed capability.

Before handoff, remove common AI-draft signals: generic rhetoric, repeated buzzwords, symmetrical but artificial lists, overlong assertion titles, invented-looking metrics, and excessive title capitalization. Preserve the organisation's established workstream language; do not introduce new categories when a familiar structure is available.

## Produce the Word briefing

Use Word Heading 1 for every intended slide, in the form `Slide N — [assertion headline]`. Before the slide headings, include:

- Presentation objective
- Audience and decision required
- Executive takeaway
- Proposed storyline
- Explicit exclusions
- Desired tone and level of detail

Under each slide heading, include only the material needed to build it:

- **Purpose / key message:** the single thing the audience should understand or decide.
- **Supporting content:** concise, source-grounded points with all qualifiers retained.
- **Evidence and sources:** facts, figures, and direct source links; clearly state assumptions and gaps.
- **Required exhibit:** specify the needed table, comparison, timeline, process flow, chart, or other visual and the fields it must contain.
- **Notes / anticipated questions:** only when useful for the presenter or a sensitive decision.

For executive updates where the audience needs to be re-oriented, begin with a context-and-objectives page that maps the discussion into a small number of numbered sections. Carry the relevant section identifier into every subsequent assertion title (for example, `2. Lighthouse learning:` or `4.3 Team enablement:`). This page is a navigation device: retain the organisation's established workstream language and do not create a second, competing taxonomy.

Start with this adaptable default flow: executive summary and recommendation; current situation and why it matters; evidence and findings; options and trade-offs; recommended course of action; risks/dependencies/mitigations; decisions required; owners and next steps. Combine, reorder, or remove steps where the source material makes that clearer; do not force an eight-slide deck.

## Handoff standard

End with a numbered slide inventory that maps each slide to its intended exhibit and source status. Ensure the document can stand alone as the primary content source for a PowerPoint build. Once the storyline is approved, hand it to `$wk-ppt-create` with the WK reference deck; this skill does not create, lay out, or revise PowerPoint slides.
