## States with No Individual Income Tax

The following states do not levy a broad-based individual income tax on wages (as of 2025):

- **Alaska** — no individual income tax
- **Florida** — no individual income tax
- **Nevada** — no individual income tax
- **New Hampshire** — taxes only interest and dividend income (phasing out)
- **South Dakota** — no individual income tax
- **Tennessee** — no individual income tax (Hall Tax fully repealed)
- **Texas** — no individual income tax
- **Washington** — no wage income tax (capital gains tax exists at state level)
- **Wyoming** — no individual income tax

## Multi-State Filing Notes

**Nonresident returns**: If a filer earns income in a state where they don't live, they generally must file a nonresident return in that state. Common situations: commuting workers, remote workers physically present in another state, RSU vesting while working in a different state.

**Credit for taxes paid to other states**: The resident state typically allows a credit for income tax paid to another state on the same income, preventing double taxation. Always compute this credit when filing in multiple states.

**Reciprocity agreements**: Some neighboring states have reciprocity agreements where residents of one state working in the other only pay taxes to their home state. Key pairs include PA/NJ, MD/DC/VA (partial), and IL/WI. Check current-year status — agreements can change.

**Part-year residents**: Filers who moved during the year file part-year returns in both states, allocating income between the periods.

## Key State-Specific Notes

**New Jersey**:
- NJ does not recognize the federal deduction for traditional 401(k) contributions — NJ wages are generally higher than federal wages. NJ AGI starts higher.
- NJ has a pension/IRA exclusion for taxpayers over age 62 with income below the threshold.
- NJ property tax deduction/credit available for homeowners and renters.

**New York**:
- NY City (NYC) residents pay a separate NYC income tax in addition to NY State tax.
- NY charges a "Metropolitan Commuter Transportation Mobility Tax" (MCTMT) for self-employed workers earning more than $50k in the MCTMT zone.
- NY IT-203 is the nonresident/part-year return; NY IT-201 is for full-year residents.
- NY allows a college tuition credit for NY State-registered schools.

**California**:
- CA does not conform to many federal provisions (no federal standard deduction, no SALT cap, different capital gains treatment).
- CA Form 540 for residents; CA 540NR for nonresidents.
- CA SDI (State Disability Insurance) withholding shown on W-2 Box 14 — fully deductible on California Schedule CA.

**Pennsylvania**:
- PA has a flat income tax rate (no brackets).
- PA does not allow most federal deductions; uses a different income base.
- Philadelphia residents pay a separate City Wage Tax.
