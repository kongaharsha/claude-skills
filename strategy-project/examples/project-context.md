# Project Context — Project Atlas
*Last updated: 2026-04-10*

## Why This Project Exists

The company is seeing early signs of saturation in its core market. A new adjacent segment — mid-market healthcare IT — has been growing at 18% CAGR and several competitors have already made moves. Leadership wants a rigorous, decision-ready assessment of whether to enter, how to enter, and what it would take to win.

This is not an exploratory exercise. The board expects a go/no-go recommendation with a supporting investment case by end of Q2.

## The Core Question

Should we enter mid-market healthcare IT, and if so, what is the highest-conviction path to a defensible position within 18 months?

## Major Workstreams

- **Market Sizing** — define the addressable market, identify the most attractive segments, and quantify the revenue opportunity under different penetration scenarios.
- **Competitive Positioning** — map incumbents and emerging players, understand positioning, and identify where differentiation is realistic.
- **Partnership Options** — evaluate build vs. buy vs. partner, identify potential partners, and assess the economics and control trade-offs.
- **Financial Model** — build the investment case with unit economics, scenario analysis, and a clear view of what needs to be true for this to work.

## Key Strategic Tensions

- **Speed vs. rigor** — leadership wants to move fast, but the decision is large and partially irreversible. Need to balance pace with analytical depth.
- **Build vs. partner** — building gives control but takes time; partnering is faster but creates dependency. No obvious right answer yet.
- **Cannibalization risk** — some mid-market segments overlap with our existing enterprise business. Need to size this honestly.
- **Talent gap** — the company has limited healthcare domain expertise internally. This shapes both the go-to-market and the partnership calculus.

## Operating Principles

- Lead with the business question, not the analysis method.
- Quantify where possible; label uncertainty explicitly.
- Every workstream should connect back to the go/no-go decision.
- Favor a clear point of view over a neutral summary when the evidence supports it.
