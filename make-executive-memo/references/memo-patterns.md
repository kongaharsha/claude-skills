# WK / Harsha Executive Memo Patterns

Use this reference when drafting or restructuring senior-leadership memos.

## Core Voice

- Polished-direct, senior, concrete, and decision-led.
- Start with the issue and the decision, not a long preamble.
- Use phrases like "This memo seeks alignment on..." and "With alignment on these decisions..."
- Distinguish strategic context, implications, proposed approach, and decisions.
- Use "we" for enterprise actions and "the team" for execution.
- Avoid motivational filler, generic transformation language, and over-explaining.

## Default Memo Architecture

Use this structure for most ELT memos:

1. **Purpose / Introduction**
   - One short paragraph on why the memo exists.
   - State the decision needed early.
   - If useful, name the 2-3 decisions that unlock next steps.

2. **Strategic Context**
   - Explain what changed externally or internally.
   - Describe why the current approach is no longer sufficient.
   - Keep the frame objective and business-backed.

3. **Implications**
   - Translate the context into what it means for the company / division.
   - Name the trade-offs and why incremental change may not be enough.

4. **Goals**
   - Define what the initiative should accomplish.
   - Use 4-6 bullets, each tied to growth, focus, speed, quality, governance, or resource allocation.

5. **Tenets / Lessons Learned**
   - Use when the memo asks leaders to align on a transformation or operating model.
   - Include principles such as decision-led governance, divisional ownership, lighthouse proof points, common standards, and change management.

6. **Proposed Approach**
   - Use 2-4 phases or workstreams.
   - For each, state the action and the output.
   - Avoid overbuilding the plan before leadership has aligned.

7. **Decisions And Next Steps**
   - End with explicit decisions.
   - Tie each decision to what it unlocks.
   - Close with the path from alignment to action.

## Pattern 1: ELT Decision Memo

Use when leadership needs to approve a program, scope, operating model, funding, or next phase.

Section sequence:

- Introduction
- Goals
- Proposed approach
- Decisions and next steps
- Appendix, if needed

Opening pattern:

> This memo asks [leaders / ELT] to align on whether [initiative] should move forward. Concretely, [number] decisions would unlock immediate next steps:

Decision bullets:

- Confirm whether [problem / initiative] is worth solving now
- Align on the initial scope: [option A] or [option B]
- Align on the delivery model: internal, external-supported, or hybrid

Closing pattern:

> With alignment on these decisions, the team can begin [next step 1], [next step 2], and [next step 3].

## Pattern 2: Strategy North Star Memo

Use when defining the future position, right to win, or enterprise strategic direction.

Section sequence:

- Executive Summary
- Market Context
- How Work / Customer Needs Are Changing
- Product And Platform Vision
- Moats / Differentiation
- Strategic Choices
- Implications And Next Steps

Style:

- Lead with an inflection point.
- Define the future control point.
- State the north star in one bold sentence.
- Explain the architecture or model in 3-5 components.
- Connect the vision to durable moats.

North-star pattern:

> We will [own / operate / become] the leading [platform / control layer / operating system] for [work domain], becoming the environment where [work] is initiated, coordinated, completed, and governed.

## Pattern 3: Transformation Memo

Use when proposing a change program across product, GTM, AI, operating rhythm, or capabilities.

Section sequence:

- Introduction
- Goals
- Tenets and lessons learned
- Proposed approach
- Decisions and next steps
- Appendix

Useful language:

- "This is not an incremental process change; it requires a new operating model."
- "Prove the model with lighthouse successes."
- "Put the divisions in the lead while establishing common standards centrally."
- "Shift from activity / backlog management to outcome ownership."

Decision pattern:

- Align on the overall transformation approach
- Nominate senior representatives / owners
- Stand up a small enablement or coordination team

## Pattern 4: Governance / Operating Rhythm Memo

Use when redesigning forums, MDMs, QBRs, operating cadences, executive reviews, or decision processes.

Section sequence:

- Introduction
- Goals
- Tenets and lessons learned
- Proposed approach
- Cadence
- Content and agenda
- Decisions and next steps

Useful principles:

- Make meetings decision-led, with decisions articulated 48 hours before the forum.
- Align cadence to purpose: operational review, strategic initiative deep dive, or performance assessment.
- Integrate product / technology / AI updates into the business narrative rather than treating them as separate updates.
- Include leading indicators and risks, not only backward-looking performance.

## Pattern 5: Portfolio / Resource Allocation Memo

Use for product rationalization, divestiture, sunset, migration, strategic posture, and investment prioritization.

Section sequence:

- Introduction
- Goals
- Proposed approach
- Phases
- Decisions and next steps

Core frame:

- Improve growth quality
- Simplify the portfolio story
- Free management bandwidth, tech capacity, and investment
- Create a fact base and decision lens
- Translate approved decisions into execution plans

Decision framework dimensions:

- Strategic fit
- Value creation potential
- Growth / margin / cash contribution
- Investment requirements
- Complexity and execution risk
- Customer / technology / people implications

Avoid starting with a full bottom-up P&L build unless the decision truly requires it. Prefer a pragmatic first pass that identifies obvious core, obvious exit, and edge-case products.

## Pattern 6: Competitive Intelligence Memo

Use for competitor updates, market signals, external intelligence, and implications.

Section sequence:

- Executive Summary / Key Takeaways
- What Changed
- Why It Matters
- Competitor Moves / Market Signals
- Implications For Us
- Recommended Actions / Monitoring Signals
- Sources

Source categories:

- Press releases and product blogs
- Earnings calls and investor presentations
- Analyst notes
- Customer interviews / field feedback
- Expert networks
- Market databases
- Product demos and public documentation

Make the memo decision-relevant. Do not stop at "competitor X launched Y"; translate into product, GTM, pricing, partnership, or roadmap implications.

## Language Patterns

Purpose:

- "This memo outlines..."
- "This memo seeks alignment on..."
- "This memo asks [leaders] to align on..."
- "Concretely, [number] decisions would unlock immediate next steps."

Context:

- "[Company / division] starts from a strong position..."
- "These strengths remain foundational, but the market structure around us is evolving quickly."
- "The historical momentum case is increasingly under pressure."
- "Existing advantages remain necessary, but they are no longer sufficient."

Implication:

- "Bottom line: incremental change will not be sufficient."
- "Competing effectively will require a materially faster operating cadence."
- "We must concentrate resources behind a smaller number of high-impact priorities."

Approach:

- "We propose a pragmatic, sequenced effort."
- "The effort should not begin with..."
- "Instead, the program should first align on..."
- "The work could be structured in three phases..."

Close:

- "With alignment on these decisions, the team can begin..."
- "Collective execution against these priorities will determine how successful we are."
