# WK Strategy Slide Pattern Library

This library is based on 488 slides from WK strategy decks covering UTD executive updates, UTD KPI dashboards, eOriginal growth strategy, TAA HCM SME SteerCo, and TAA Professional AI in Audit final readouts.

## Pattern Mix Observed

- KPI dashboards and quantitative pages: common in weekly updates and market/customer sections.
- Matrix/table pages: the dominant readout structure for evidence, prioritization, competitors, roadmap, and customer findings.
- Dense readout pages: market maps, system/genome diagrams, capability maps, and process flows.
- Options/recommendation pages: strategic pathways, go/no-go choices, priority targets, and investment decisions.
- Executive summary pages: concise answer-first framing, often with grouped evidence blocks.
- Agenda/section pages: simple tables or section markers used to orient long decks.
- Visual exhibit pages: screenshots, product examples, market maps, competitive landscapes.
- Next-step pages: short action lists, activity timelines, decisions needed.

## 1. Executive Summary

Use for: opening a readout, weekly leadership update, SteerCo recap, or decision pre-read.

Structure:
- Title: `Executive Summary | [answer in one sentence]` or a single `Exec summary` title for short updates.
- Body: 3-5 evidence blocks or a compact table.
- Left-to-right logic: market/customer/product/financial implication, then decision or ask.
- Highlight only the strongest insight per block.

Best practices:
- Put the "so what" in the title or first body line.
- Use blue outlines or light gray panels to group points.
- Reserve yellow for assumptions or "watch item" emphasis.
- Keep the page readable as a standalone email attachment.

## 2. KPI Dashboard / Weekly Update

Use for: activation, engagement, funnel, churn, revenue, TCV, subscriber base, roadmap progress, customer/NPS metrics.

Structure:
- Top title is a factual takeaway, not `Metrics`.
- Use 2-4 KPI zones or charts, each with a short label and explicit time frame.
- Include source/date and notes at the bottom.
- Add status color only where it changes interpretation.

Best practices:
- Put the key number near the chart, not buried in text.
- Use small multiples for funnel or trend comparisons.
- Call out variance and direction with concise annotations.
- Keep axes and legends minimal but not ambiguous.

## 3. Matrix / Table Readout

Use for: competitors, product capabilities, roadmap, target screening, workstreams, customer findings, feature comparison, priority lists.

Structure:
- Title bar across the top.
- Table occupies most of the page, with header row in blue/pale blue or gray.
- One row per option, competitor, workflow, customer type, or initiative.
- Rightmost column often captures implication, priority, or recommendation.

Best practices:
- Use 8-10 pt text for dense tables; 7 pt only for secondary labels.
- Make row heights consistent.
- Use fills sparingly: blue for structure, yellow for assumptions, green/orange/red for status.
- If a table has more than 6 dense columns, split into multiple slides or create a summary table plus appendix.

## 4. Options / Recommendation

Use for: growth pathways, strategic choices, build/buy/partner, market entry, investment tradeoffs.

Structure:
- Compare 2-4 options in columns or rows.
- Use consistent criteria: vision, target customers, strategic moat, investment needed, risks, open questions.
- End with a clear recommended path or decision needed.

Best practices:
- Keep criteria labels identical across options.
- Do not bury the recommendation in the table; state it in the title or a callout.
- Use option names that are concrete business choices, not vague themes.
- Separate "what must be true" assumptions from current facts.

## 5. Dense Market / System Map

Use for: market structure, lending/audit/HCM genome, workflow maps, ecosystem maps, capability landscape.

Structure:
- Title states the market truth or implication.
- Use swimlanes, stages, or ecosystem layers.
- Keep the map centered and use labels as navigation.
- Add a bottom implication strip when the map is complex.

Best practices:
- Use consistent box sizes for comparable entities.
- Use lines/arrows only when relationships matter.
- Group with light fills and thin outlines; avoid decorative shapes.
- For very dense maps, create a simple orientation page first and move detail to appendix.

## 6. Chart Analysis

Use for: survey results, market sizing, funnel drop-off, revenue trend, ROI, TAM/SAM/SOM, prioritization scatter.

Structure:
- Title states the conclusion.
- Chart is the main object; explanatory text is secondary.
- Add 1-3 annotations directly near the relevant data.
- Include source and base size for survey charts.

Best practices:
- Prefer simple bar/line/scatter/waterfall charts.
- Use WK blue as the default series color and contrast colors only for exception, gap, or risk.
- Label units in the chart title or axis.
- Do not over-grid; make the chart easy to read at slide-show distance.

## 7. Agenda / Section Divider

Use for: long readouts, SteerCo decks, appendix transitions.

Structure:
- Minimal page with `Agenda`, `Appendix`, or section title.
- For agendas, use a compact table with current section highlighted.
- Keep it spare; this is navigation, not analysis.

Best practices:
- Reuse the same agenda page through the deck when marking progress.
- Highlight current section with WK blue or light blue fill.
- Avoid large decorative visuals.

## 8. Next Steps / Decision Ask

Use for: end of update, workplan, open decisions, post-meeting action list.

Structure:
- 3-5 actions grouped by owner, timing, and dependency.
- For project plans, use a table or simple timeline.
- Include decisions needed from leadership if relevant.

Best practices:
- Make verbs concrete: `Validate`, `Align`, `Launch`, `Confirm`, `Negotiate`.
- Include due dates or meeting milestones when known.
- Keep the page less dense than evidence pages.

## 9. Executive Workshop / Leadership Session

Use for: short leadership sessions, offsites, working meetings, alignment discussions, or pre-read decks that need to guide conversation rather than serve as a final evidence pack.

Structure:
- Cover page with meeting topic, audience, date, and 1-line purpose.
- Agenda or objectives page with 3-5 discussion blocks.
- Context page: why this topic matters now, with only the minimum proof.
- 3-5 discussion pages, each with one governing question or takeaway.
- Decision / discussion ask page: explicit decisions, tradeoffs, and next steps.

Best practices:
- Keep pages lighter than a final readout: 2-4 major content zones per page.
- Use large readable titles and headers; do not use dense evidence-pack typography.
- Put discussion questions in clearly labeled callouts, not buried in tiny text.
- Use tables, lanes, or structured blocks for talking points. Avoid many loose text boxes.
- For executive sessions, prioritize clarity and polish over fitting every point on the slide.
- If a page feels like a notes page, convert it into an appendix page or speaker notes.
